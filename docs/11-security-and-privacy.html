Nizam-e-Qanoon Security and Privacy

A threat-driven, privacy-minimising security architecture for Pakistani legal intelligence: capability separation, matter isolation, verifiable legal evidence, constrained AI, recoverable operations and an auditable account of every privileged act.

§0Mandate
& invariants

Security is an architectural property, not a gateway in front of an unsafe system

Document 11 owns confidentiality, integrity, availability and privacy across the platform. It narrows implementation choices in every earlier document. If a convenience conflicts with an invariant here, the invariant wins and the release gate in Document 10 blocks shipment.

InvariantEnforced propertyProof required
SEC-1Anonymous requests have no account, matter, transcript, artefact, analytics event or content-bearing log writer.Composition-root test, database diff, object-store diff, provider-payload capture and log scan all show zero content persistence.
SEC-2Every authenticated object access is authorised by actor, role, matter, object and current workflow state; PostgreSQL RLS is a second boundary.Cross-user matrix, direct-ID adversaries and forced-RLS database tests produce zero disclosure.
SEC-3Retrieved law is untrusted DATA_ONLY. Models have no network, database, tool, secret, publication or signing capability.Prompt-injection suite cannot cross a port boundary or produce a privileged side effect.
SEC-4Only approved immutable source bytes can become published legal evidence; citation handles resolve server-side.Digest, provenance, maker-checker and signed-manifest chain validates from source to client.
SEC-5Secrets, credentials, legal-sensitive content and raw queries never enter URLs, ordinary logs, crash reports, metrics or training data.Schema allowlists, redaction canaries and storage scans pass before release.
SEC-6Privileged acts require named people, least privilege, separation of duties and audit-before-effect where risk demands it.Role graph proves no author can self-approve corpus, rule, model, offline pack or production release.
SEC-7Failure is closed: no model-only answer after retrieval failure; no unsigned pack; no stale authority after revocation; no silent security degradation.Fault injection exercises each forbidden fallback.
SEC-8Security evidence is itself restricted, minimised and access-audited.Audit readers are separate roles; exported evidence is scoped, encrypted and time-limited.

Legal boundary

This is an engineering specification, not a claim of statutory certification or legal advice. Controls are chosen from enacted Pakistani law, official policy/guidance and international standards, with their status kept distinct. Product policy may deliberately exceed a current legal minimum.

§2Governance
& risk

One risk register connects public harm to an accountable control owner

Governance follows NIST CSF 2.0’s Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover functions. Threat modelling combines STRIDE for system failures with privacy/harm analysis: dignity, chilling effects, exposure of family or criminal matters, economic loss, professional prejudice and exclusion caused by language or access.

Every feature carries: data-flow change, abuse cases, data classes, purpose, processors, retention/deletion behaviour, authz rules, logging schema, incident signals and test evidence. High-risk changes—identity recovery, provider transfer, legal drafting, bulk export, staff tooling, new corpus sources—require threat-model review before implementation.

Derived chunks, embeddings, caches, exports and backups inherit the highest source class and purpose. A deletion tombstone identifies an internal object without retaining deleted content. Public law combined with a user’s query becomes legal-sensitive because the association can reveal intent.

§5Privacy
lifecycle

Purpose limits the data before encryption protects it

Each field must have a named product purpose, class, lawful/applicability rationale approved by counsel, allowed readers/processors, deletion trigger and maximum retention. “Improve AI”, “analytics” and “security” are not sufficient purposes without a specific use and necessity test.

StateRequired behaviourForbidden shortcut
CollectJust-in-time notice in English/Urdu; collect the minimum; separate optional improvement consent.Bundled consent or pre-ticked training reuse.
UsePolicy check binds purpose and matter; provider sees request-local pseudonym only.Using legal matters for general model training or staff curiosity.
StoreLegal-sensitive content encrypted; metadata minimised; user-facing retention state.Raw query in path, URL, metric label or exception.
ShareExplicit recipient/scope; short-lived signed export; disclosure event audited.Public bearer link or silent support/provider access.
DeleteActive row/object/cache/index removal, wrapped-key destruction, tombstone and backup expiry.Deleting UI row while embeddings, cache or export remain searchable.
RecoverBackups retained for inherited 14-day recovery window; restored data re-enters deletion queue before service.Restoring deleted content into active user access.

Only the inherited operational-log and backup windows are fixed here: content-free operational logs up to 14 days and recovery backups up to 14 days. Account, matter, draft and audit schedules require a separately approved retention register before production. Legal hold is exceptional, scoped, documented, access-restricted and removed when its basis ends.

5.1User control

Authenticated users can view stored categories, export their matter in a documented format, correct account data, delete individual artefacts or the account, withdraw optional reuse, and see pending/complete deletion. Identity verification for a request must be proportionate; support cannot ask for the very sensitive facts being protected. These are product commitments and forward-compatible privacy engineering, not a claim that the draft PDP Bill is enacted.

§6Anonymous
proof

Anonymous means technically unable to remember

buildAnonymousGraph() {
  return QueryApp(
    corpus = PublishedCorpusReadPort,
    retrieval = EphemeralRetrievalPort,
    generation = MinimisingProviderPort,
    clock = MonotonicClock,
    telemetry = ContentFreeMetricsPort,
    accounts = ABSENT, matters = ABSENT, artifacts = ABSENT,
    transcripts = ABSENT, objects = ABSENT, auditContent = ABSENT);
}
  • No stable browser/device identifier, fingerprint or cross-request correlation. Security rate limits use short-lived, coarse, rotating signals.
  • Reverse proxy excludes query strings and bodies; application errors carry a random request ID only.
  • Provider contracts/configuration prohibit training and retained prompts where the selected service supports it; payload conformance is tested.
  • Memory buffers expire on completion, cancellation or timeout. Swapping, core dumps and content-bearing crash capture are disabled for workers.
  • Voice is processed request-locally; anonymous voice is disabled if that cannot be guaranteed.

The proof is a five-surface diff—database, objects, queues/caches, telemetry and provider capture—before and after canary requests, plus a dependency-graph assertion that no persistence adapter is reachable.

OAuth/OIDC implementations follow RFC 9700: exact redirect matching, PKCE, issuer/audience validation, no implicit-flow design, sender constraint where practical, and refresh-token rotation. Passwords, if ever introduced, use a maintained Argon2id library and parameters benchmarked to the deployment; home-grown cryptography is prohibited.

§8Authorisation
& RLS

Authorise the object and workflow state, then make the database disagree by default

  • Opaque IDs reduce guessing but never replace authorisation. Every read, mutation, export, share, citation artefact and stream reconnect checks ownership/membership.
  • Sensitive tables use ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY and FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY, explicit USING/WITH CHECK, default deny, non-owner runtime roles and migration tests.
  • Background jobs carry a signed scope envelope; there is no generic “system user”. Bulk/admin roles are separate, time-bound and audited.
  • Support starts metadata-only. Unmasking content needs user-approved support where possible or an incident/legal basis, JIT grant, reason and notification policy.
§9Cryptography
& keys

Keys express boundaries and make deletion operational

SurfaceRequired primitive/patternKey rule
NetworkTLS 1.3 preferred; TLS 1.2 only with an explicit compatibility profile; HSTS externally.Automated certificate renewal and expiry alert.
Legal-sensitive fields/objectsVersioned authenticated encryption (AEAD) with envelope encryption and context-bound associated data.Per-user or per-matter DEK; wrapped by environment KEK kept outside DB/object store.
Passwords if introducedArgon2id through a maintained library.Per-record salt; optional pepper held separately; rehash on successful sign-in.
TokensAt least 128 bits CSPRNG entropy; store one-way digest where lookup is needed.Purpose, audience and expiry bound; immediate family revocation on replay.
Corpus/offline/releaseEd25519 detached signature over canonical manifest; SHA-256 content digests.Separate offline root, release signer and online service keys; clients pin trusted public keys.
MobileOS Keychain/Keystore-backed non-exportable key where available.No secrets in preferences, logs, source, bundle or shared clipboard.

Every ciphertext records algorithm suite, key ID/version, nonce, associated-data schema and creation time. Rotation supports dual-read/single-write then measured rewrap. Destruction of a user/matter DEK wrapper provides crypto-erasure for encrypted backup copies while active rows/objects/derivatives are deleted; backup expiry completes physical retirement. This is a product design, not a statutory claim.

Pilot practicality

The pilot may use an OS-protected offline KEK service with tightly permissioned runtime identity, encrypted off-box recovery and dual-control rotation. Moving to a managed KMS/HSM is triggered by multi-host deployment, regulated-customer requirement, key-operator scale or inability to meet recovery/rotation evidence—not by fashion.

10.1Provider qualification

The registry records service/region, subprocessors, retention/training settings, transfer path, deletion behaviour, abuse-monitoring exception, encryption, breach terms, availability, model/version pinning and audit evidence. Payload tests prove configuration rather than trusting marketing. The provider receives request-local pseudonyms, minimum facts, approved evidence and no user phone/account/matter ID. A provider change is a security, privacy and evaluation release.

§11Legal evidence
integrity

A hostile PDF never becomes executable authority

  1. Fetch only approved official HTTPS origins, enforce redirect/size/type limits, record headers and hash raw bytes before parsing.
  2. Quarantine and scan; parse/OCR in an unprivileged, networkless sandbox with CPU, memory, time and output limits. File extension never establishes type.
  3. Preserve immutable raw evidence and extractor/model/container versions. Normalised text remains linked to page coordinates and raw hash.
  4. Run coverage, structure, temporal and legal-relation gates. High-risk schedules and status-changing relations require legal maker-checker review.
  5. Publish atomically from a dedicated role; application/model roles have no index write. Sign canonical corpus/offline manifests and retain rollback.
  6. Continuously compare source and published digests. Deletion/revocation cascades to chunks, embeddings, caches and packs.

Hashes and signatures prove byte relationships and signer control; they do not prove that the source is legally operative, current, correctly interpreted or admissible. Those are separate temporal, authority and legal-review findings.

§13Platform
& supply chain

The single-VPS pilot is small, not trusted

  • Default-deny host firewall; public exposure limited to reverse proxy; SSH keys only, no root login, restricted administration source where practical; PostgreSQL/cache/object services bind private/localhost interfaces.
  • Separate non-root service identities, read-only images/filesystems where practical, dropped capabilities, resource limits, health checks and no Docker socket in application containers.
  • Secrets arrive at runtime with least-readable permissions, never baked into image or Git. Startup fails on placeholder/overbroad secrets; access and rotation are audited.
  • Security patch policy is risk-based: internet-exploitable critical exposure has an owner and emergency window; every exception expires. Time sync, disk/backup capacity and certificate/key expiry alert before failure.
  • Encrypted off-box backups, 14-day recovery window, monthly scratch restore and golden evaluation. Restore credentials are separate from production; deletion queues run before a restored service opens.

13.1Software, model and data supply chain

Every release has lockfiles, dependency and container/model/corpus digests, SBOM, vulnerability and secret scans, licence inventory, test evidence and signed provenance. CI uses ephemeral least-privilege credentials; untrusted pull-request code cannot access release secrets. Images are pinned by digest; mutable tags do not deploy. SLSA 1.2 supplies the provenance vocabulary, while NIST SSDF 1.1 structures secure development. Conformance levels are not claimed unless independently evidenced.

§14Audit &
telemetry

Three evidence streams have different purposes and readers

StreamContainsMust not contain
Operational telemetryRoute template, coarse outcome, latency, status family, version IDs, random request ID.Query/body, names, phones, tokens, matter/object IDs, citation text, model prompt/response.

Audit events are appended through a narrow service role and chained/batched with digests; signed checkpoints are stored separately. This makes unauthorised alteration detectable, not metaphysically “immutable”. Reads, searches, exports, retention changes and audit-policy changes are audited. Clocks synchronise to UTC and retain source/uncertainty. Operational logs expire within 14 days; audit/legal replay retention comes from the approved register.

§17Delivery
& proof

Build the controls in dependency order

  1. Foundation: compliance/data registers, owners, threat model, secret handling, hardening baseline, approved sources/providers.
  2. Isolation: separate anonymous graph, account/matter schema, object authorisation, forced RLS, session/recovery and staff MFA.
  3. Evidence integrity: sandboxed ingestion, immutable provenance, signed publish/offline pipeline, model citation resolver.
  4. Privacy/crypto: field minimisation, envelope encryption, export/deletion, backup-aware crypto-erasure and provider egress conformance.
  5. Detection/recovery: allowlisted telemetry, restricted audit/checkpoints, alerts, incident runbooks, restore/key/deletion drills.
  6. Assurance: ASVS/API/MASVS/RAG traceability, Doc 10 adversaries, independent penetration test and signed production decision.

17.1Production definition of done

A production release is not done until all SEC invariants have automated evidence; all sensitive tables are enumerated and forced-RLS tested; anonymous five-surface proof passes; provider payloads and retention settings are captured; source/release/offline signatures verify; deletion and restore complete; secrets/keys rotate; incident contacts and applicability register are current; high-risk findings are closed; and Document 10’s security/privacy critical-event ledger is empty.

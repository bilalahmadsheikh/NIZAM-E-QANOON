## Review (24 Nov 2026, reviewer: claude, read)

Each row was decided by reading its passages and the candidate law's title in the corpus. Only `alias` rows reach
`law_alias`; the others are kept here as the reason the citation stays unresolved.

| Decision | Rows | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| alias | 81 | The printed name, read in its passages, names one held law (abbreviation, spelling, former name, short name) |
| ambiguous | 34 | More than one law could be meant: an act number, a schedule Article, a name without its province or year, or a short name seen in one judgment only |
| not_held | 30 | The law named is not in the corpus, or the printed year is not the held law's |
| foreign | 13 | Indian, English or UK statutes cited comparatively |
| not_a_law | 11 | Grammar remnants: CPC Orders, footnote numbers, a cricket board's constitution |
| historical | 3 | Pre-1947 and interim constitutional texts not held |

Rules applied:
- **Two judgments, or a year.** A name becomes an alias only if it is seen in two or more judgments, or printed with
  a year that makes it unique. A short name one judgment defines for itself is that judgment's own.
- **A year that disagrees is not corrected.** "Specific Relief Act, 1977" and "Transfer of Property Act, 1982" stay
  unresolved (D-127).
- **Copies.** An alias names the law. When the corpus holds the same law twice (the 34-law compilation document 4497
  repeats the National Accountability Ordinance, the Civil Servants Act and the Service Tribunals Act), the
  resolver's own preference picks the copy, and the others become alternates.

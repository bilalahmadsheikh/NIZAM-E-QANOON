Where the project stands

Seven layers, built bottom-up

Each layer only talks to the one below it. We build in that order because a search engine over an untrustworthy corpus gives numbers that mean nothing. The bottom two layers are done; the third is about a third of the way.

How a PDF becomes a citable provision

From an official portal to a search result, in seven steps

Each step's output is stored, so any answer can be traced back to the exact printed page it came from. Counts are what the database holds now.

STEP 1Observe10,397source observations: what each government portal listed, whensource_observation
STEP 2Store bytes10,191distinct files, fingerprinted by SHA-256blob
STEP 3Extract135,025pages · 2.85 M text blocks with their position on the pagedocument · page · text_block
STEP 4Identify the law10,342laws, with name, number, year, dates, status, territoryinstrument
STEP 5Segment1,172,523provisions in a tree: Part › Chapter › Section › Clauseprovision
STEP 6Date the text1,114,702versions: which words were law between which datesprovision_version
STEP 7Index for search1,132,245chunks with keyword indexes and meaning vectorschunk

Quality gates sit between the steps. A law is released only when its text matches an independent extractor at 99.9% or better, its printed table of contents agrees with its parsed sections, and nothing on its pages is left unplaced. Laws that fail stay out. Today none fails.

What is in the corpus

Laws by place, and whether each is still law

Current laws are served as operative law. Not current laws were repealed, lapsed (an Ordinance not passed in time) or replaced; they are kept and served for dates before they ended. Historical laws are pre-1947 texts or laws of places outside Pakistan, served only for history.

Inside one law

A law is a tree of provisions, and the provision is what gets cited

We never cite a "chunk" or a page. We cite a provision: section 302(b), Article 25(2), clause (iii) of a proviso. Each provision has a stable address in the tree, so re-processing a law never breaks a citation that was already given.

The shape of a law

Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code as our database stores it, with the kind of each node.
law Pakistan Penal Code (1860) └ chapter XVI └ section 302 Punishment of qatl-i-amd ├ clause (a) … ├ clause (b) … └ clause (c) … └ proviso Provided that … each node → versions: the words in force from date A to date B

A version is the wording valid over a date range. The database makes overlapping ranges for one provision impossible to store, so "what did section 302 say on 1 Jan 2010?" has exactly one answer.

How laws connect

Laws amend, repeal and empower each other

An Act says "as prescribed", and the prescription is in Rules made years later. Answering from the Act alone gives a confidently incomplete answer, so links between laws are stored as data.

The five rules nothing may break

Invariants: a design that breaks one is wrong, however well it scores

No made-up citationsSection numbers, dates and links come from the database. The language model only names identifiers.
No answer without proofSearch returns either grounding or "I can't answer this". There is no third option, and no fallback to the model's memory.
Every answer replayableThe question, filters, results, scores, prompt and model version are kept for every answer.
Cite provisionsAbove the storage layer nothing refers to a chunk. Re-chunking never breaks a stored citation.
Law as at a dateEvery search carries a date and reads the operative view, never the raw table.
Search, the layer we are building now

Keyword search plus meaning search finds three-quarters of the right law

We measure on golden questions with known answers: 200 so far, split into a dev half we tune on and a test half scored once. Recall@50 is the share of the right provisions that appear in the top 50 results. The reranker, next, decides which of the 50 reach the answer.

What is left

The road from here, in the order it has to happen

  1. Finish the GPU embedding. 24 slices of Qwen3-Embedding-8B are running on Kaggle; then load, measure against bge-m3, keep it only if it wins.
  2. Reranker and gate. A cross-encoder over the top 50, then the abstention gate with thresholds calibrated on dev. Target recall@50 0.90, NDCG@10 0.75.
  3. Finish the golden set. 300 more questions, including the 10% that our corpus cannot answer, annotated by two people with agreement (κ) reported.
  4. Answer generation. The self-hosted, fine-tuned 27–32B model, citing identifiers only, with every claim checked against its source.
  5. Services, API, apps. Limitation periods, court-fee and stamp-duty calculators, drafting; the HTTP contract; the offline phone pack and answer screens.
  6. Corpus growth. Case law (no judgments yet), 1,025 scanned AJK and GB laws that need OCR, and the other 11 link types.
Every law, by name

The law register

All 10,342 laws are listed in the repository at docs/law-register/: one readable table per place, and laws.csv with 35 columns per law. The columns cover name and its evidence, kind, number, year, enacted and commenced dates with their basis, status, currency and reason, territorial reach, repeals, provision count, page count, source URL and file fingerprint. It is generated from the database by tools/law_register.py, so it never drifts from what the system serves.
